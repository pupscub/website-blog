---
title: "Thoughts on Minimalism"
date: "2024-01-10"
excerpt: "Why less is often more, especially in software design and daily life."
---

Minimalism isn't about austerity — it's about clarity. Whether I'm pruning a machine learning pipeline or tidying a
living space, I keep rediscovering how removing friction reveals the work that matters.

The same mindset applies to product development. Tight scopes make it easier for teams to ship, observe, and learn.
When every feature has a story, a metric, and a sunset plan, the roadmap stays honest. I regularly revisit my backlog
with a simple rubric: does this change move a key metric, unlock learning, or delight users in a measurable way? If not,
it probably doesn't belong.

To stay grounded I rely on a rotating set of rituals — weekly reviews, lightweight KPIs, and the occasional “digital
cleanup day” where I archive docs, close dead branches, and refactor small annoyances that crept in.
