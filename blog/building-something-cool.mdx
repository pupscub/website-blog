---
title: "Building Something Cool"
date: "2024-01-15"
excerpt: "Thoughts on the creative process and shipping products that people actually want to use."
---

Building something meaningful is rarely about chasing a perfect idea. It's about working closely with the people who
will use what you create, keeping feedback loops short, and staying humble enough to change course when the signal
demands it.

I like to start any new project with a “walking prototype” — a lightweight explainer, sketch, or even a Loom where I
talk through the flow. It gives collaborators something concrete to react to while keeping the investment low enough to
pivot quickly. Once the direction is clear, I iterate on a real build with telemetry hooks from the start so we can
watch usage patterns in real time.

The best launches I've been part of paired curiosity with discipline: we experimented aggressively, but always measured
outcomes and documented the lessons. That rhythm makes it easier to know when to double down, when to rethink, and when
to ship confidently.
